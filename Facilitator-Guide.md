## 📖 Pre-Workshop Preparation (2-3 weeks before)

### Week -3: Planning & Setup

**Administrative Setup:**
- [ ] Secure workshop venue/classroom
- [ ] Arrange 4 hours/day for 5 days
- [ ] Ensure stable internet (critical!)
- [ ] Set up projector + laptops for demos
- [ ] Create student roster and attendance tracker
- [ ] Print materials (glossary, activity workbooks, checklists)

**Technical Preparation:**
- [ ] Confirm institution-approved AI chat and document-grounded tools
- [ ] Verify current access requirements, data settings, and usage limits
- [ ] Prepare an offline/backup demonstration; do not require personal or paid accounts
- [ ] Treat Day 4 automation accounts and integrations as optional
- [ ] Test internet speed (minimum 5 Mbps)
- [ ] Test video playback and screen sharing
- [ ] Download backup screenshots in case of internet issues
- [ ] Create shared folder with all materials

**Content Preparation:**
- [ ] Print/digitize the available daily content files and activity handouts
- [ ] Prepare presentation materials; the current package does not include slide decks
- [ ] Select approved CS course sources for the Day 3 project
- [ ] Prepare a synthetic-data workflow diagram for Day 4
- [ ] Record backup demo videos for each project

**Student Preparation:**
- [ ] Send pre-workshop email with:
  - [ ] Workshop schedule and expectations
  - [ ] List of institution-approved tools and optional account instructions
  - [ ] What to bring (laptop, charger, notes)
  - [ ] AI Glossary to familiarize themselves
- [ ] Create shared folder/LMS for materials
- [ ] Set up feedback/Q&A mechanism

## 🎯 Classroom Management Tips

### Creating a Safe Learning Environment

**Important:** Many students may feel intimidated by AI. Set the right tone:

**Day 1 Opening Statement:**
```
"This workshop is for B.Sc. Computer Science students. You do not need prior
experience with AI, APIs, or databases. If you have programming experience,
you can try the optional coding extensions, but everyone can participate in
the core activities.

AI is for everyone. There are no 'dumb questions' here. 
If you're confused, others probably are too. Please ask!

By the end, you will have built and tested two practical AI-assisted
deliverables and learned how to check their limits. Let's do this together!"
```

### Managing Different Learning Speeds

**Students Finishing Early:**
- "Now, improve your work. Make it more polished."
- "Help a classmate who's stuck."
- "Explore this tool feature I mentioned."
- "Write reflection on what you've learned."

**Students Struggling:**
- Pair with stronger student
- Provide step-by-step written guide
- Reduce scope (fewer requirements)
- Offer office hours for support
- Provide video tutorial to watch later

**Students Questioning Value:**
- "You'll use these skills in your job, I promise."
- "This is how real teams work today."
- "Show them a job posting requiring AI skills."
- "Let them test it on their own homework."

## ⏰ Time Management Strategy

### The 4-Hour Daily Structure

**Format:** 20% Theory + 60% Hands-On + 20% Activities

**Example Day 1 (4 scheduled hours including a 15-minute break):**
- 0:00-0:30 (30 min) → AI, ML, and GenAI [concepts]
- 0:30-1:15 (45 min) → How GenAI works: 15 min concepts + 30 min guided demo
- 1:15-1:30 (15 min) → Break
- 1:30-2:30 (60 min) → Explore approved AI tools [guided hands-on]
- 2:30-3:15 (45 min) → Code, media, and presentation examples [guided hands-on]
- 3:15-4:00 (45 min) → Interactive challenge [activity]

Across each day's 225 teaching minutes, target 45 min concepts, 135 min
guided hands-on work, and 45 min activity/project work.

**Critical Rule:** If you're talking for >45 min straight, students zone out. Mix it up!

### Pacing Adjustments

**If Running Behind:**
- Cut the least essential part of the day's content
- Do hands-on instead of lecture (learning is better anyway)
- Focus on 2-3 key concepts, not all details
- Save advanced topics for optional reading

**If Ahead of Schedule:**
- Do more hands-on practice
- Have students create more ambitious projects
- Run the competitions earlier
- Do peer learning (students teach each other)

## 👥 Facilitator Dos & Don'ts

### ✅ DO:

1. **Start and End on Time**
   - Respect student schedules
   - Shows professionalism
   - Models good habits

2. **Live Demo Everything First**
   - Show it before asking students to do it
   - Pause and let them catch up
   - Show multiple approaches

3. **Circulate During Hands-On**
   - Walk around classroom
   - Check on struggling students
   - Answer questions immediately
   - Celebrate successes

4. **Use Real Examples**
   - Use their college, their careers, their lives
   - Makes AI feel relevant
   - Shows practical value

5. **Make Mistakes Intentionally**
   - Demonstrate bad prompt → good prompt
   - Show what happens when AI hallucinates
   - Show troubleshooting process
   - Normalizes learning from failure

6. **Encourage Experimentation**
   - "Try something wild!"
   - "What if we asked it this way?"
   - "See what happens if..."
   - Praise creative thinking

7. **Provide Multiple Formats**
   - Video, slides, printed, verbal
   - Some people read, some watch, some listen

### ❌ DON'T:

1. **Assume Prior Knowledge**
   - Explain even "basic" things
   - Remember: No one knows this stuff
   - It's new to everyone

2. **Rush Hands-On Time**
   - Let students explore
   - Wait for everyone to catch up
   - Better to do less and understand fully

3. **Read Slides Verbatim**
   - Slides are prompts, not content
   - Speak naturally
   - Engage your audience
   - Tell stories

4. **Ignore Struggling Students**
   - Check in regularly
   - Offer support early
   - Pair with stronger students
   - Adjust on the fly

5. **Get Lost in Technical Details**
   - Stick to student-level explanations
   - Skip the advanced theory
   - Focus on "how to use" not "how it works"
   - Save deep dives for optional reading

6. **Present AI as Magic or Perfect**
   - Be transparent about limitations
   - Show hallucinations happening
   - Emphasize verification need
   - Build critical thinking

7. **Forget About Students' Lives**
   - Connect to their careers
   - Use their examples
   - Show how this helps them
   - Make it personal

## 📋 Daily Facilitator Checklist

### Before Each Day

- [ ] Review today's content and activities
- [ ] Test approved demo tools; confirm students can participate without accounts
- [ ] Download backup materials (offline)
- [ ] Prepare any external videos or links
- [ ] Print any needed handouts
- [ ] Arrange classroom layout
- [ ] Test projector, audio, internet
- [ ] Set up backup internet (phone hotspot)
- [ ] Charge any needed devices
- [ ] Prepare attendance sheet
- [ ] Have extra notebooks/pens available

### During Each Day

- [ ] Start on time, end on time
- [ ] Take attendance
- [ ] Review previous day's key concepts (5 min)
- [ ] Preview today's learning objectives (2 min)
- [ ] Do live demo first, then students follow
- [ ] Circulate during hands-on activities
- [ ] Monitor time, give warnings before transitions
- [ ] Check understanding via questions, not just lectures
- [ ] Capture good student examples for later reference
- [ ] Take notes on struggling areas
- [ ] End with reflection on the day
- [ ] Preview next day's topic (2 min)

### After Each Day

- [ ] Collect any submissions
- [ ] Send daily feedback/summary
- [ ] Note which students need extra support
- [ ] Adjust next day's pace based on today
- [ ] Recharge for tomorrow!

## 🎓 Supporting Different Student Backgrounds

### For Students with Different CS Experience
- Do not assume students already know Python, APIs, databases, or machine learning.
- Use examples from programming fundamentals, data structures, databases,
  networks, testing, and student projects.
- Offer optional code extensions; keep the core activity tool-agnostic.
- Encourage students to explain and test generated code rather than copy it.

### For Anxious Students
- Remind them that the core projects do not require coding; coding extensions are optional
- Show very easy wins early
- Provide extra support materials
- Pair with confident peer
- Celebrate every success

### For Very Advanced Students
- Provide optional deeper readings
- Suggest they help peers
- Give them challenge problems
- Discuss cutting-edge AI research
- Show them programming path if interested

## 📞 Handling Common Situations

### Situation: Student Can't Get Account Access

**Quick Solution:**
- Use the instructor's projected demonstration or prepared offline screenshots; do not share passwords
- Share your screen and have them follow along
- Let them complete at home later
- Provide video tutorial for after workshop

---

### Situation: Internet Goes Down

**Prevention:**
- Download offline materials beforehand
- Record videos for offline viewing
- Have screenshots/demos ready
- Test backup internet daily

**During Outage:**
- Use offline activities (brainstorm, discuss)
- Go through demo slides
- Have group discussion
- Switch to different activity

---

### Situation: Student Gets AI Hallucination

**Teaching Moment:**
- "Perfect! This is exactly what I wanted to show!"
- Explain hallucination concept
- Verify the information together
- Show how to detect it
- Make it a learning opportunity

---

### Situation: A Student is Bored

**Reasons & Solutions:**
- Too easy? → Give advanced projects
- Don't see value? → Connect to their goals
- Prefer different style? → Offer alternatives
- Social needs? → Change seating/groups

---

### Situation: Student Worried About Jobs

**Be Honest:**
- "Yes, AI will change some jobs"
- "But it also creates new jobs"
- "AI-literate professionals are in high demand"
- "Learning now puts you ahead"
- "Most jobs will use AI, not be replaced by it"
- Show job postings requiring AI skills

## 🎯 Post-Workshop

### End-of-Workshop Checklist

- [ ] Collect all submissions (both projects)
- [ ] Give written feedback on each project
- [ ] Give certificates/completion verification
- [ ] Collect feedback survey from students
- [ ] Send thank you email
- [ ] Share resources for continued learning
- [ ] Offer office hours for Q&A
- [ ] Create alumni community/Discord (optional)

### Feedback Survey for Students

Ask students to rate:
- Content difficulty (too easy / just right / too hard)
- Content pacing (too slow / just right / too fast)
- Hands-on time (enough / just right / wanted more)
- Instructor clarity (did they explain well?)
- Relevance (useful for you?)
- Overall satisfaction (1-5 rating)
- What was best?
- What could improve?
- Would you take another workshop?

---

### Feedback Survey for Facilitators (Self-Reflection)

- What went well?
- What could improve?
- Which days need adjustment?
- Which activities were most engaging?
- Which students struggled most?
- How could I better support struggling students?
- Did I stay on time?
- Was content at right level?
- Would I change anything for next workshop?

## 💡 Pro Tips from Experience

1. **Build Relationships**
   - Learn student names by Day 2
   - Ask about their goals
   - Make it personal
   - Students learn better when they trust you

2. **Create Community**
   - Encourage peer help
   - Celebrate group achievements
   - Build collaborative spirit
   - Make it fun!

3. **Be Authentic**
   - Show your own learning journey
   - Admit what you don't know
   - Be genuine and enthusiastic
   - Students can tell if you care

4. **Adapt On The Fly**
   - Watch student faces
   - If confused, slow down
   - If bored, speed up
   - Flexibility > rigidity

5. **Connect to Their Goals**
   - Ask what they want to do
   - Show how AI helps
   - Make it relevant
   - Inspire their futures
