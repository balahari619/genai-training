# AI Glossary for Students

**Simple, student-friendly explanations of AI terms used in this workshop**

---

## A

**AI (Artificial Intelligence)**
Computer programs that perform tasks that normally require human intelligence. Examples: recognizing faces, recommending movies, playing chess.

**Algorithm**
Step-by-step instructions for a computer to solve a problem or complete a task. Like a recipe for cooking, but for computers.

**API (Application Programming Interface)**
A defined way for software systems to request information or actions from one another. Access may require credentials, permissions, and may incur costs.

**Automation**
Setting up computer processes to run automatically without human intervention. Example: Auto-reply email when you're on vacation.

---

## B

**Bias (in AI)**
When AI makes unfair decisions based on patterns in training data. Example: If trained mostly on male faces, AI might not recognize women well.

**Chatbot**
Software designed to simulate conversation with humans. Examples: Customer service bots, ChatGPT.

---

## C

**ChatGPT**
AI chatbot made by OpenAI that can understand and generate human-like text responses.

**Claude**
AI chatbot made by Anthropic, known for being very careful and accurate.

**Conversational AI**
AI that can have back-and-forth conversations with humans, understanding context and responding appropriately.

---

## D

**Dataset**
A collection of data used to train AI models. Example: A dataset of 1 million labeled photos of dogs.

**Deep Learning**
Type of machine learning using neural networks (inspired by human brain) to learn from data.

---

## E

**Embedding**
A way to represent words or concepts as numbers so AI can process them. Like giving each word a unique fingerprint.

---

## F

**Fine-tuning**
Further training a model on a narrower dataset or task. It is different from giving a prompt or attaching documents, and requires appropriate data, tools, and expertise.

**Hallucination (AI)**
When AI confidently says something false or makes up information that sounds believable. A known limitation of current AI.

---

## G

**Generative AI**
AI that creates new content (text, images, code, etc.) based on learned patterns and its input. Examples include text, image, audio, and code generation tools; specific products and features change.

**Gemini**
Google's AI chatbot/assistant that can process text, images, and documents.

---

## H

**Hallucination**
See definition under "F"

**Hyperparameter**
Settings that control how AI learns from data. Like the heat setting on a stove - different settings produce different results.

---

## I

**Input**
The data or information you give to AI. Example: The question you ask ChatGPT.

**LLM (Large Language Model)**
A type of model trained to process and generate language. ChatGPT is a product that can provide access to one or more models; product and model are not interchangeable terms.

---

## J

*No common AI terms starting with J*

---

## K

**Knowledge Base**
An organized collection of information used to support search or question answering. A knowledge base can improve relevance, but does not guarantee accurate answers.

---

## L

**Language Model**
An AI model that processes or generates language. Many language models generate text token by token, conditioned on the available context.

**LLM**
See "LLM" under "I"

---

## M

**Machine Learning (ML)**
Type of AI where computers learn from examples instead of being explicitly programmed. Like learning to recognize faces by seeing many examples.

**Model**
The trained computational system that processes inputs and produces outputs. A chat product may provide access to different models.

---

## N

**Natural Language Processing (NLP)**
Technology that helps computers understand and work with human language (reading, writing, translating).

**Neural Network**
Computer system inspired by how human brains work, used in deep learning.

**NotebookLM**
A document-based AI workspace that can answer questions about selected sources. It is not automatically a custom, standalone chatbot application; verify answers against the cited source.

---

## O

**Output**
The response or information AI gives you. Example: ChatGPT's answer to your question.

**Overfitting**
When AI learns the training data too perfectly but doesn't work well on new, different data. Like memorizing a test instead of understanding concepts.

---

## P

**Parameter**
A variable in an AI model that influences how it makes decisions. Billions of parameters = more complex AI.

**Prompt**
The instruction or question you give to AI. The better your prompt, the better the response.

**Prompt Engineering**
The skill of writing effective prompts to get better AI responses. The main focus of Day 2 of this workshop.

---

## Q

**Quantization**
Reducing the size of an AI model by using fewer numbers/precision, making it faster but slightly less accurate.

---

## R

**Retrieval-Based AI**
A system that searches a collection and returns relevant stored items, such as documents or pre-written answers. It is distinct from generation, though retrieval can be combined with a language model.

**RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)**
A design pattern in which a system retrieves relevant information and supplies it to a generative model as context. Retrieval and generation can both fail, so source checks are still needed.

---

## S

**Sentiment Analysis**
AI analyzing whether text is positive, negative, or neutral. Example: Reading customer reviews and categorizing them.

**Source**
The original document or reference that AI used to answer your question. Good AI tools show you the sources.

**Supervised Learning**
AI learning where humans provide examples with correct answers. Like learning with a teacher who provides answers.

---

## T

**Token**
A unit of text processed by a model; it may be a word, part of a word, or punctuation. Token boundaries vary by model and are not the model's "thoughts."

**Training**
The process of teaching an AI model using examples and data.

**Transfer Learning**
Using knowledge from one task to help learn another task more quickly. Like using math skills to learn physics.

---

## U

**Unsupervised Learning**
AI learning without labeled examples, finding patterns on its own. Like organizing books into categories without being told what the categories are.

---

## V

**Vector**
A mathematical representation of data that AI can process. Like converting words into numbers.

**Verification**
Checking AI's answers to make sure they're correct. Important because AI can hallucinate!

---

## W

**Workflow**
A series of automated steps that happen in sequence. Example: Form submission → Process data → Send email.

---

## X

*No common AI terms starting with X*

---

## Y

**Yield**
The output or result you get from AI. Similar to output.

---

## Z

*No common AI terms starting with Z*

## 🎯 Key Terms to Remember

### Concepts
- **AI vs ML vs GenAI:** Three related but different technologies
- **Training:** How AI learns from data
- **Hallucination:** When AI makes confident but false statements
- **Prompt:** How you communicate with AI
- **Model:** The trained AI system itself

### Practical Tools
- **Chatbot:** AI you talk to (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude)
- **LLM:** Large Language Model, powers modern AI
- **Prompt Engineering:** Skill of asking AI the right way
- **Knowledge Base:** Documents AI learns from
- **Workflow:** Automated series of steps

### Technical (Optional)
- **Training:** Process of teaching AI
- **Parameter:** Setting that affects AI behavior
- **Bias:** Unfair patterns in AI decisions
- **Embedding:** How AI represents words numerically

## 📝 Student Vocabulary Quiz

**Test yourself! (Answers below)**

1. What is the difference between AI and ML?
2. What does "hallucination" mean in AI?
3. Why is verification important when using AI?
4. What is prompt engineering?
5. Explain how a knowledge-based chatbot works.

---

### Answer Key

1. **AI vs ML:** AI is the broad field (making computers intelligent). ML is one way to do it (learning from data). Not all AI uses ML.

2. **Hallucination:** When AI confidently provides false information because it's designed to generate text that flows well, not necessarily true text.

3. **Verification:** Important because AI can hallucinate. You should check facts, especially for academic or professional use.

4. **Prompt Engineering:** The skill of writing clear, specific prompts so AI gives you useful answers. Better prompts = better responses.

5. **Knowledge-Based Chatbot:** AI trained on specific documents. When you ask questions, it searches those documents and provides answers based on them, usually with source citations.
